  1. Who's involved

    A purchase connects four parties through the network: the cardholder and their issuer, the merchant and its acquirer. Blue arrows are messages; green ones are money.

    Doc 01: What is a card network · Doc 02: Participants and roles

  2. Authorization: asking the issuer

    At checkout the terminal builds an 0100 authorization request. The network routes it by BIN to the issuer, which answers with an 0110 and places a hold. No money moves yet.

    Take apart an 0100, field by field Build one in the POS

    Doc 03: Stage 1 · Doc 04: Authorization deep dive

  3. Clearing, settlement and funding

    That night the acquirer sends the final amounts (clearing). The next day banks pay each other one net amount (settlement), and the acquirer pays the merchant (funding). Disputes can follow for months.

    Doc 05: Clearing and settlement · Doc 08: Exceptions and disputes

  4. Where the money goes

    The merchant pays a merchant discount rate. Most of it is interchange to the issuer; the network keeps a small fee; the acquirer keeps the rest.

    Doc 06: Economics and fees

  5. The card number

    The card number routes the payment: its BIN picks the issuer and its last digit is a Luhn check. Chips and phones add a one-time cryptogram, and wallets swap the number for a token.

    Doc 07: Cards, BINs and tokens · Doc 09: Risk, fraud and security

    On top of the core rails sit card products, payment flows, value-added services and data. Each tag shows where simple-network builds it.
